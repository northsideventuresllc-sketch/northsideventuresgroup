Sheet A-101 · PhilosophyShokunin · The Craft Ethic

Why The Hand Matters

Making Is Not a Skill. It Is an Ethic.

Arch11 is led by architects who came to the discipline as builders — craftspeople who learned the language of materials through direct experience. Like the Japanese shokunin, we hold that making carries a responsibility: to understand, to refine, and to execute with integrity.

That lineage shapes everything. Our architects stay with a project from the first sketch to the final moments of construction — checking the joint the hand drew. "We've tried to gather people who are makers," says co-founder E.J. Meade. "Cabinetmakers, people who make wedding cakes. They have an engagement with the tactile qualities of the world."

Each line, joint, and threshold should reflect the precision, care, and intention with which it was imagined. Nothing leaves the studio that the hand hasn't touched.

Sheet A-105 · StewardshipAIA 2030 Commitment Signatory

Sustainability Is Not an Add-On

Stewardship Is the Practice

At Arch11, sustainability was never a checklist item. "Sustainable building practices are just inherent in each design," Meade says. "It's not something that's added — it's just part of good architecture."

We begin with the land: light, topography, climate, vegetation. Buildings are oriented to invite winter warmth and summer shade, positioned to catch daylight and prevailing winds, and detailed to last — because the greenest building is the one never torn down.
